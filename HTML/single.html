01 / JOURNEY / CORDILLERA

The long
road north

WORDS / INA SANTOSPHOTOGRAPHS / LEON RIVERA28 AUG 2026 / 12 MIN

Three days through rain, mountain roads and towns that disappear into cloud before noon.

The road leaves the valley without ceremony. One moment there are houses, bright signs and tricycles moving between market stalls; the next, everything narrows to wet asphalt and pine.

By six in the morning, cloud had erased the upper slopes. We drove with the windows open, listening for water in the gullies and the low mechanical complaint of buses climbing somewhere ahead.

Before the weather turns

Every bend rearranged the same elements: rock, road, river and a horizon that appeared only briefly. At a roadside store above Atok, the owner set down three cups of coffee and pointed to the ridge.

“The mountain is not hiding. It is asking you to wait.”

So we waited. Rain ticked against the roof, a dog slept beneath a bench, and slowly the valley returned.

17.0832° N

Weather moving east
Visibility / 800 m

The distance between towns

Maps flatten a journey into a line. On the ground, distance is measured differently: in landslides cleared, coffee stops taken, conversations begun because the rain became too loud.

Past noon, children walked home beneath umbrellas the color of traffic signs. Terraces stepped down into the cloud. The road kept its patient course north.
